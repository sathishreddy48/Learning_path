How to use this site
  • Start with The GenAI Round. This round is scored differently from system design and differently again between companies, and the commonest way to fail it is to answer a systems question with a framework name. That page is the rubric.
  • Then the two foundations. Everything after assumes you can talk about tokens, context and sampling precisely, and that you know what a tool call is on the wire.
  • Then Building, in order. RAG is the most-asked design question; evals are the follow-up most candidates have no answer to; agents tie both together.
  • Then Internals. This is where candidates are separated — far more people have built a RAG pipeline than can say what a causal mask does or why generation is memory-bandwidth-bound.
  • Code samples are Python. Copy grabs the block.
  • Interview angle boxes say what is actually being graded in that section, and the sentence to say out loud.
  • Visited state lives in your browser only, separate from the C#, System Design, DSA, LLD and Playbook sites.
Build something before you read this

Of all four rounds, this is the one where reading is least sufficient. Interviewers in AI rounds probe for whether you have actually run the thing — what your chunk size was and why, what your retrieval recall was, what broke when you changed models. Build one RAG pipeline end to end with a real corpus and a real eval set before you rely on any of these pages. The specifics you collect doing that are the answer to half the questions here.

Questions worth rehearsing out loud

Not pages here, but they recur across loops and each one is answered by material on this site. Practise saying each in ninety seconds:

  • "Design a system that answers questions over our internal documents." — the default RAG prompt; the follow-up is always evaluation.
  • "Your RAG system returns a wrong answer. Debug it." — the answer is to separate retrieval failure from generation failure, and say how.
  • "Would you fine-tune for this?" — usually the correct answer is no, and the credit is in why.
  • "How do you stop it hallucinating?" — grounding, structured output, and the honest admission that you reduce rather than eliminate.
  • "This agent will send emails. What do you change?" — the approval-gate question, dressed up.
  • "Estimate the cost of serving this." — tokens, batching, and total-versus-active parameters.
