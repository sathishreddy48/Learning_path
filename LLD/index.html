How to use this site
  • Start with The LLD Round. This interview is scored differently from system design, and most candidates who fail it fail on the clock or the requirements rather than on the design. That page is the rubric and the timetable.
  • Then Principles & Patterns. Six patterns answer almost every prompt; the page is about picking one under time pressure, not about the catalogue. The catalogue lives on the C# patterns page.
  • Then the worked designs, in order. Each one is a full round: clarifying questions, the model, complete code, the concurrency story, and the follow-ups that prompt attracts.
  • Every code sample has a C# | Python tab bar. Copy grabs whatever tab is showing.
  • Interview angle boxes say what is actually being graded in that section, and the sentence to say out loud.
  • Visited state lives in your browser only, separate from the C#, System Design and DSA sites.
Work the problem before you read it

These pages give away the answer in the first screen. Reading a worked design is close to worthless compared with attempting it — set a 45-minute timer, build it, and then read. The gap between what you produced and what is here is the only part of this site that will change your score.

Suggested order

Go in sidebar order. The two foundations pages (1–2) are short and everything after assumes them. Parking lot (3) is the fullest treatment and establishes the vocabulary — invariants, seams, check-then-act — that the rest refer back to, so do not skip it even if the prompt looks beneath you. After that the four designs are independent and you can take them in any order.

Other prompts worth practising

Not worked here, but they come up and they reuse the same moves. For each, predict the seam before you start:

  • Notification dispatcher — Observer plus a channel Strategy; the follow-up is retries and ordering.
  • Logger — Chain of responsibility over levels, with appenders behind an interface.
  • Tic-tac-toe, then chess — the real question is whether adding a new piece is a new class or an edit.
  • Library / inventory system — entity modelling plus a reservation state machine, like parking with reservations.
  • Text editor with undo — the Command pattern, and the one prompt where it is genuinely the answer.
  • Snake and ladders, deck of cards, ATM — short warm-ups; useful for drilling the first ten minutes.
